01 — The Challenge

How might we design a solution that helps brands manage unsold product in a customer-focused way?

Every brand on Flipkart had the same problem: their bestsellers got all the attention, and everything else stayed invisible. A shopper would find a popular product, buy it, and leave — never knowing the same brand made dozens of other products that could have been relevant to them.

This wasn't just a user experience problem. It was a platform economics problem with a three-sided consequence — for brands, for Flipkart, and for shoppers.

Brands
The business relied heavily on top-performing products, while the broader catalogue stayed hidden from users.
Flipkart
A significant share of GMV was left untapped because many catalogue items were never surfaced to shoppers.
Shoppers
Potentially relevant products were overlooked simply because they did not appear prominently in the shopping journey.
02 — Research & Analysis

Understanding the problem through the people who live it.

We ran in-house user interviews, behavioral data analysis, and competitive benchmarking to understand not just that products were invisible — but why. The research sharpened the problem considerably.

This wasn't a content problem — it was a navigation and placement problem. Shoppers had no natural path to a brand's wider catalog once they landed on any single product page. Brands were guests in a crowded room, not hosts of their own space.

03 — 5W & H Framework

Defining the problem
with precision.

Before designing anything, we framed the problem using the 5W & H method — ensuring every design decision was anchored to a real person, in a real context, with a real consequence.

Who
Brand-conscious Flipkart shoppers
Users who want to explore a specific brand and its full product range — not just find one bestseller and leave.
What
Cluttered results, no brand presence
Cluttered search results, unclear official brand presence, fragmented product discovery, and repeated searching across categories.
Where
Search results & product browsing
The user is on Flipkart — mainly navigating through the search results page and the product browsing flow.
When
After finding one product
The problem occurs when users search for a brand, browse results, select a product — and then want to explore other categories from the same brand.
Why
Trust, discovery, and revenue at stake
It affects trust, slows down product discovery, reduces cross-sell opportunities, and creates a frustrating shopping experience.
How
Search → browse → repeat
Users search category + brand name, browse listings, pick one product, add it to cart — then repeat the entire process for every other category.
04 — Competitive Audit

What Myntra and Amazon got right — and left open.

We benchmarked against Myntra and Amazon to understand how leading platforms handle brand discovery — and where the gaps were that Flipkart could own.

Section Myntra Amazon
Platform TypeFashion-first, brand-led shoppingBroad marketplace, strong variety
StrengthsStrong visual design, curated feelPowerful search, organised catalog
WeaknessesOnly one entry point to brand store via searchBrand storytelling feels diluted at scale
GapsBrand store visibility missed if user doesn't notice itBrand-led navigation isn't always seamless
OpportunitiesAdd entry points from product browser & product pageImprove official brand visibility across categories
Key Insights
Myntra currently provides a single main entry point to the brand store via search.
Amazon has a product page entry point such as "Visit Store" from the brand name.
Our design opportunity: improve discoverability by introducing three entry points — search, product browser page, and product page.

06 — Problem Definition

Pains mapped to Needs —
the design brief.

Each pain point from research was mapped to a corresponding user need. These pairs became the brief for every design decision.

Pain
Shoppers have no path to a brand's wider catalog from a product page
Need
A dedicated brand space with a coherent navigation path to the full catalog
Pain
Non-popular products never surface alongside bestsellers
Need
A curated page where all products have equal discovery potential
Pain
Brand identity is invisible — one brand looks identical to another
Need
A branded destination with hero area, identity content, and storytelling modules
Pain
Moving from brand browsing to product and back breaks context entirely
Need
A recoverable shopping path — seamless transit between storefront and product
Pain
Brands have no persistent presence — every visit starts from scratch
Need
A permanent storefront accessible from multiple entry points across the platform
The First Idea — Rejected
Idea: Feature products in "Deal of the Week" homepage section
× Only 2–3 cards per day — every other category competes for the same space
× Manual updates required every week — an operational burden that doesn't scale
× Maximum 2 brands simultaneously — doesn't address the catalog-wide problem
07 — Solution

Six friction points.
One destination.

Not a filtered product listing. Not a generic brand tab. A dedicated storefront — simultaneously a marketing surface and a shopping surface — where brands could finally be hosts of their own space inside Flipkart.

01
Branded Hero Area
Identity zone with brand name, logo, tagline, and visuals that establish who the brand is before selling begins.
02
Curated Collections
Products organised by collection and use case — not just popularity — giving every item equal discovery potential.
03
Multiple Entry Points
Accessible from product pages, search results, and category browser — discovery happens naturally, not intentionally.
04
Recoverable Path
Shoppers move from store to product and back without losing context. The brand's world remains reachable at every stage.
05
Brand Storytelling
Banners, seasonal content, and messaging that give brands a voice beyond product specs — marketing within shopping.
06
Design System
Reusable component library that allowed 65+ stores to be built at speed without sacrificing visual consistency.

11 — Reflection

What I learned building this

"
Data sharpens judgments.
It doesn't replace it.

The "Deal of the Week" idea failed not because it was wrong to explore — but because we stress-tested it honestly and found it solved the symptom, not the cause. Understanding why a problem exists before deciding how to solve it is the most important lesson I carried out of this project.

Every decision was grounded in research and validated by outcomes. But data gave us the map. Judgment helped us read it — and design something that worked not just for one brand, but for the sixty-five that came after.
